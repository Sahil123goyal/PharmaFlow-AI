1 Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This document specifies the software requirements for the Smart Airport Management Sys-
tem (SAMS) , Version 2.0. SAMS is an academic web application that centralizes selected
airport operational records and coordinates follow-up work between authorized airport depart-
ments.
Version 2.0 extends the scope of SRS v1.1. The v1.1 prototype gave a single administrator
a central interface for flights, luggage, visitors, staff and transportation. Version 2.0 turns this
into a role- and department-based system: each department works in its own workspace on
the records it owns, other departments see the information they need for coordination, and
significant operational events automatically notify the right people, create follow-up tasks and
leave an accountable history.
This SRS is a requirements specification. It does not assert that the newly described features
are already implemented; the status of every requirement is stated explicitly.
1.2 Intended Audience and Reading Suggestions
 Course faculty and evaluators — to assess scope, feasibility and verifiability. Chapters
1 and 2 give the complete picture.
 Development team (Group 5) — to plan and implement the system. Chapter 3
(System Features) and Chapter 5 (data, interfaces and phases) are the most relevant.
 Testers — to derive test cases from requirement IDs (Section 5.3).
 Future maintainers — to understand roles, departments, data ownership and extension
points.
Readers new to the project should start with the one-line summary below, then read Section 2.2
(users and departments) and Section 2.6 (example workflow).
SAMS is a role-based airport management system that connects passenger journeys
and airport departments through shared operational data, targeted notifications,
accountable incident timelines, and overdue-task escalation.
1.3 Project Scope
Airport operations involve many departments — flight operations, baggage handling, ground
transport, maintenance and passenger services — that depend on each other’s information.
When each department keeps isolated records, a problem in one area (for example, a baggage
issue on a flight) is communicated late or inconsistently, nobody is clearly responsible for the
follow-up, and there is no reliable record of who did what and when.
SAMS addresses this with one web application and one shared backend and database. Public
flight and transport information is available without login. Passengers may optionally create an
account, register their journeys and receive targeted notifications. Staff and administrators sign
in through a common entry point and are redirected to the workspace appropriate to their role
and department.

Objectives
1. Centralize flight, luggage/baggage, visitor, staff and transportation records, retaining the
existing v1.1 modules where practical.
2. Enforce server-side authorization by role, department, resource scope and permit-
ted fields.
3. Provide separate role-aware workspaces within one React application, not separate code-
bases or login systems.
4. Link operational events to targeted departmental notifications and action items .
5. Provide an incident and audit timeline showing important changes, decisions, acknowl-
edgements and outcomes.
6. Escalate overdue high-priority tasks to the responsible supervisor under configurable
rules.
7. Provide passenger-specific journey and baggage notifications without exposing another
passenger’s private information.
In Scope (initial expanded MVP)
 Existing authentication and management modules from v1.1, preserved and extended.
 Public, read-only flight, announcement and transport information.
 Passenger accounts, registered journeys and targeted in-app notifications, using demon-
stration data.
 Fully implemented workspaces for the first three departments: Flight Operations, Bag-
gage Handling and Ground Transport .
 Maintenance and Passenger Services represented in the design and added as time
permits (phased).
 Central administrator workspace, supervisor permissions, approval workflow, event pro-
cessing, action-item tracking, audit timeline and overdue-task escalation.
 Testing with seeded demonstration records and one complete cross-department incident
scenario.
Out of Scope (initial expanded MVP)
 Live airline, airport, RFID, air-traffic or baggage-scanner integrations.
 Real ticket booking, payments, check-in or boarding-pass issuance.
 Real airline operational decisions such as guaranteed connection rebooking.
 SMS delivery and production notification providers (email may be a later extension).
 AI-based prediction, microservices and large-scale production deployment.

2 Overall Description
2.1 Product Perspective
SAMS evolves the v1.1 prototype rather than replacing it. It keeps the three-layer architecture
and the technology stack, and adds new modules on top of the existing ones. The system
remains a modular monolith: all modules share one backend and one database, while access
is controlled by the API.
1. Presentation layer— one React single-page application containing the public portal, the
passenger My Journey pages, the department workspaces and the administrator workspace.
2. Application/API layer — a Node.js/Express REST API. Every protected request
passes through authentication and authorization middleware before reaching controllers
and services. New services handle events, impact rules, notifications, action items, ap-
provals, escalation and audit logging.
3. Data layer — MongoDB accessed through Mongoose. The browser never accesses the
database directly.
Presentation layer — one React SPA (Vite, React Router, Axios, Tailwind, Recharts)

2.5 Design and Implementation Constraints
 Technology stack: the v1.1 stack is retained (React, Vite, React Router, Axios, Tailwind
CSS, Recharts; Node.js, Express, Mongoose, JWT, bcryptjs; MongoDB; Git/GitHub)
unless a blocker is found.
 Architecture: a modular monolith with one frontend, one API and one database — not
microservices and not separate applications per department.
 Preserve working code: existing working login and CRUD features shall be preserved
and extended, not needlessly replaced.
 Data: no live airport or airline feed is assumed; demonstrations use seeded or manually
entered data.
 Secrets: database credentials and JWT secrets are held in environment variables and are
never committed to the repository.
 Notifications: in-app only for the MVP; no SMS or external delivery providers.
 Team and time: a semester-long project by four students; Maintenance and Passenger
Services are phased accordingly.
 Cost: only free and open-source tools and free-tier hosting.
2.6 Example Workflow: A Baggage Issue
The following scenario is the reference cross-department workflow for the MVP and the basis of
the end-to-end acceptance test (Figure 2.5).
1. Baggage Handling staff report a baggage issue and link it to a flight.
2. SAMS stores the issue and records an operational event, in the same transaction where
supported.
3. Impact rules identify the relevant recipients, such as Baggage Handling and Flight Oper-
ations.
4. Relevant people receive in-app notifications, and follow-up action items are created.
5. Flight Operations assesses any flight impact. A baggage report does not automatically
change the flight schedule.

6. If a sensitive change is needed (e.g. a delay), it follows the approval policy and the decision
is recorded.
7. Only relevant passengers receive personal alerts; public pages show approved public infor-
mation only.
8. Staff record follow-up and resolution; the incident timeline preserves the full history.

5.4 Delivery Phases
1. Audit the existing source code and confirm what already works.
2. Define roles, departments and the server-side permission matrix; restrict public registra-
tion.
3. Add passenger journeys and targeted in-app notifications.
4. Complete the Flight Operations, Baggage Handling and Ground Transport workspaces
and ownership rules.
5. Add persisted events, impact rules, notifications and action items.
6. Add the incident timeline and audit history.
7. Add the approval workflow and overdue-task escalation.
8. Run the end-to-end scenarios and update documentation; then add phased departments
as time permits.
5.5 Acceptance Criteria for the Initial Expanded MVP
1. Existing core functions are audited and working functions are preserved.
2. Role and department permissions are enforced by the backend.
3. A passenger can register a test journey and receives only relevant in-app alerts.
4. A baggage issue linked to a flight produces an event and targeted department notifications.
5. Notification acknowledgement is tracked separately from task status.
6. The incident timeline shows report, notifications, decisions and resolution from persisted
records.
7. An overdue task triggers a supervisor escalation according to a configured rule.
8. A sensitive change remains pending until an authorized decision is recorded.
9. Tests show that private passenger data and unauthorized operational fields cannot be
accessed or modified.
